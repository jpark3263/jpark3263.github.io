Anytime Non-Line-of-Sight Radar Imaging with Complex-Valued Selective State-Space Models

Jinhyeok Park, Han Cho, Apurba Prasad Padhy, Saibal Mukhopadhyay
IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV), 2027

Abstract

Radar penetrates optically opaque materials, making it attractive for through-obstruction perception in time-critical settings. But radar resolution scales with aperture, so high-resolution reconstruction is slow, needing a large accumulated aperture or minutes of offline optimization. We argue that what time-critical settings need is a usable reconstruction first, from as few frames and as low latency as possible. Therefore, we recast non-line-of-sight radar imaging as a streaming, anytime task by leveraging a state-space model (SSM) that processes per-frame measurements sequentially and emits a surface estimate as data arrives. The SSM architecture gives agility: our model saturates its own quality from 2% of the aperture at millisecond processing latency, an end-to-end time under a minute against tens of minutes for the offline state of the art. A complex-valued transition A = −exp(a) + jω gives fidelity. On 44 real occluded objects, we reach quality competitive with the offline state of the art (0.70 vs. 0.73 F-score) at over an order of magnitude lower end-to-end time.
